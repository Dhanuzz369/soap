One of the first questions a new buyer asks is whether to import powder or liquid. There is no universal answer, and anyone who quotes you a single winning format for every country is guessing. This guide gives you the market factors that decide the question, so you can reason it out for your own shelf.

## Start with how people in your market actually wash

Before comparing formulas, look at how your customers do their laundry. Three habits shape demand more than any brand campaign.

- **Hand wash or machine wash.** Powder has a long history in bucket and basin washing. Shoppers scoop it, rub it into the cloth and judge it by the lather. Liquid is gaining ground in machine washing, where it dissolves fully and is easy to dose.
- **Water temperature.** Powder dissolves best in warm or hot water and can leave residue in cold water if the grade is poor. Liquid goes into cold water without any trouble, which matters where households wash in cold tap water.
- **Shopping rhythm.** If people buy a day's wash at a time from a small shop, sachets and small powder packs fit. If they stock up monthly at a supermarket, bottles and large refill pouches fit.

A short visit to local shops, or a call to your best distributor, tells you more than a general report. Photograph what is on the shelf, note the pack sizes, and write down what is sold at the till for a few coins.

## The market factors that decide it

### Climate and storage

Humidity is the main enemy of powder. In hot, damp regions powder can absorb moisture, lump and lose flow if the pack is poor. Good laminated pouches and moisture-proof cartons solve most of this, but they cost money and your warehouse still needs to be dry. Liquid does not suffer from humidity, though it dislikes strong heat and frost over a long time, and the bottle needs a reliable cap and seal.

### Washing machine penetration

Count the washing machines, not the population. Where only a minority of homes have a machine, powder aimed at hand washing will move the volume. Where machines are standard, shoppers look for low-foam front-load products and liquid in measured doses. Note that top-load and front-load machines need different foam behaviour, so check which type dominates before you choose a grade.

### Price sensitivity

Powder normally gives you more entry-level price points, since formulations with higher filler content can be sold at a low price per pack. Liquid is usually positioned as the premium, convenient choice, with a higher price per wash. If your shoppers buy mostly on price, an economy powder gives you the widest base. If your customers are upper-income households, modern trade shoppers or hotels, liquid and a fabric softener alongside it can lift the basket value.

### Freight cost per wash

Compare formats by what it costs to land one wash on your shelf, not by the cost of a kilogram or a litre. Liquid contains a large share of water, so you are paying to ship water. Powder is more concentrated, but light powder can use up container volume before it reaches the weight limit. The result depends on grade, pack and density, which is why we work out a container plan for each enquiry rather than quote a rule of thumb.

## Who should lead with which format

These are tendencies, not rules. Test them against your own shelf visit.

**Lead with powder if** your customers mostly wash by hand, you sell through small shops and kiosks, shoppers compare price per pack, or your climate and distribution are tough on liquid bottles.

**Lead with liquid if** machine ownership is high, your buyers are supermarket or online shoppers, you sell to hotels, laundries or guesthouses that value easy dosing, or you want a premium line with room for better margins.

**Lead with both if** your market is split, for example big cities moving to machines while smaller towns still wash by hand. In that case a mixed container is a cheaper experiment than guessing wrong with a full load of one product.

## Building a range around the first order

A sensible first order is rarely a single product. Consider a core item and one or two supporting items:

1. An economy hand-wash powder in small and medium packs for price-led volume.
2. A premium machine powder or a liquid in 1 L to 3 L bottles for the upgrade shopper.
3. A fabric softener to raise the sale per household, once the core range is moving.

Keep fragrance choices short at the start. Two or three well-chosen fragrances are easier to sell and to restock than a long list.

## Practical checks before you decide

- **Wash a sample the way your customers do.** Use the same water, the same cloth and the same hands or machine.
- **Check the label rules.** Some markets require specific wording, language or registration for detergents. Confirm this before artwork is printed.
- **Think about the second order.** The best first product is one you can restock easily, with packs that fit your warehouse and your distributors' trucks.
- **Do not copy a neighbour's market.** Two countries next to each other can have very different washing habits, prices and import costs.
