## Product overview

The laundry bar is still the everyday washing product in many homes where clothes are scrubbed by hand, at a river, in a basin or at a communal tap. It is compact, cheap per wash, easy to carry and needs no measuring. For importers and distributors in these markets, a reliable laundry bar is a high-turnover line that sells all year round.

## Grades and formulations

Laundry bars fall into two broad families. Soap-rich bars, typically 60-72% TFM, are built on fats and oils and are valued for hardness, slow wear and gentle behaviour on hands. Detergent bars, typically 54-60% TFM, replace part of the soap with synthetic surfactants such as LABSA-based actives, plus fillers and builders. They cost less and clean better in hard water, which is why they dominate many price-sensitive markets.

Colour and fragrance help a bar stand out on a crowded shelf. The classic blue bar uses a light tint and brightener, white is popular for a clean image, and unperfumed yellow bars carry a traditional, high-TFM positioning. Lemon and rose scents are common for detergent bars. An oxygen-active bar with a stain-removal booster suits buyers who want a premium angle.

## Packaging for export

Bars are normally wrapped in printed flow wrap, or boxed for premium lines, then packed in master cartons of 48 to 144 bars depending on weight. Because bars are dense, container weight limits can matter as much as volume, so a loading plan is shared with each quotation. Container figures vary with bar size and carton type. Unwrapped bulk bars can be supplied if you prefer to wrap locally.
