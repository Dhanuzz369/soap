## Product overview

Dish wash liquid is one of the fastest-moving household cleaning products, because it is bought again and again and used every day. A good product produces stable foam, removes cooking oil without long scrubbing, rinses cleanly and stays gentle on hands. Buyers also judge it by look and feel: the colour, the fragrance on the sponge and how far a small squeeze goes.

## Grades and formulations

Dish wash liquids are built on anionic surfactants such as LABSA and SLES, often with a co-surfactant for foam stability and a thickener to set the viscosity. Standard grades typically carry 15-25% active matter and suit mass-market retail. Concentrated grades, typically 25-30%, use less water and are popular where freight cost per wash matters or where the brand promises long-lasting value. Gel versions are thicker and are usually sold in squeeze bottles with a flip-top cap.

Fragrance is the main shelf differentiator. Lemon and lime remain the most requested in nearly every market. Orange and green apple give a softer, fruitier profile. An antibacterial lemon version carries a mild antimicrobial additive for buyers who want that claim, subject to local regulation. An unscented or sensitive-skin version with minimal dye is available for foodservice and baby-care positioning.

## Packaging for export

Retail bottles range from 250 ml to 1 L, with flip-top or screw caps and either printed labels or full shrink sleeves. Refill pouches reduce plastic and shipping weight and sell well where price is the main driver. For restaurants and hotels, 5 L cans are packed two or four per carton, and 20 L and 50 L carboys and 200 L drums serve institutional users and importers who bottle locally. Container loads vary with bottle shape and carton size, so a loading plan is shared with each quotation.
