## Product overview

Cooking leaves a film of oil on almost every kitchen surface, and over time that film hardens into a sticky, brown layer that ordinary cleaners barely touch. A kitchen degreaser is built for this task. It breaks the grease into small droplets that can be wiped away, and a good one does this without long soaking or heavy scrubbing. Demand is steady in households that cook with oil daily and in the commercial kitchens that serve them.

## Grades and formulations

Degreasers combine surfactants with alkaline builders and a solvent such as a glycol ether to cut through oil. Ready-to-use sprays typically carry 8-12% active matter and a moderate pH, which makes them safe and easy for household use. Heavy-duty concentrates typically carry 20-30% active matter and a higher pH, and are diluted from about 1:5 to 1:20 depending on how soiled the surface is. Higher alkalinity cleans faster but needs more care on aluminium and painted surfaces.

Scent matters because kitchen smells linger. Lemon and orange citrus are the usual choices and mask cooking odour well. An unscented grade is available for food preparation areas and for foodservice buyers who prefer no fragrance. Colour can be matched to your pack, and clear products with no dye are also possible.
