## Product overview

Fabric softener is an add-on purchase that grows as households adopt washing machines and want better-feeling, better-smelling laundry. It softens fibres, reduces static and creases, makes ironing easier and leaves lasting fragrance. For importers, it is a higher-margin partner product to detergent, and shoppers tend to be loyal to a favourite scent.

## Grades and formulations

Softeners are built on cationic softening agents, together with water, a thickener, preservative, colour and fragrance. Regular grades usually carry about 4-8% active matter. Concentrates carry roughly 10-15% and are sold as a premium, economical-per-wash option. Sensitive grades use lighter perfume and gentler dyes for skin-conscious households.

Fragrance is the main reason people choose one softener over another. Floral bouquet, lavender, ocean fresh and rose are the most requested scents, and perfume-boost concentrates play on long-lasting freshness. The fragrance, colour and viscosity can be adjusted within the manufacturer's range to suit your market.

## Classification note

Softener sits in a grey area for customs. It is commonly classified under 3402 50, but is often also classified under 3809 91 as a textile finishing agent. The HSN code shown is the 6-digit level, and the full 8-digit ITC-HS code is confirmed for each order so your paperwork matches your destination country's practice.

## Packaging for export

Softener bottles are usually 1 L to 3 L PET or HDPE with a measuring cap, with stand-up refill pouches alongside. Larger buyers take 5 L cans or 20 L jerrycans, and hotels and laundries may take drums. Master cartons hold 6 to 12 bottles. The liquid is dense and the cartons are heavy, so payload sets the container limit.

## Private label options

Softener is a fragrance-led category, so the most important private-label decision is the scent and its strength. Concentrate formulas allow a smaller bottle at the same number of washes.
